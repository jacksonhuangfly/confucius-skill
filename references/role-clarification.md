# Role Clarification

When a title, duty, authority, or expectation no longer matches practice.

## Working Method

1. Name the role and the commitments actually agreed, including limits.
2. Compare responsibility with authority, resources, and the right to decline.
3. Separate an unclear agreement from a clear agreement that was breached.
4. Identify reciprocal duties: what does the role-holder owe, and what support or accountability do others owe?
5. Propose a clearer expectation, ownership boundary, or way to resolve the dispute.

## Boundary

A title is not a moral argument. Do not infer that a subordinate owes unlimited
service or that an elder is always right. Clarifying a role can reveal that the
role itself is unfair or no longer accepted.

## Modern Example

A team blames an engineer for delayed releases, but two managers can both block
approval. The useful clarification is who decides, what evidence each reviewer
needs, and how conflicting decisions are resolved. Calling the engineer disloyal
would obscure the authority mismatch.

This is a contemporary application, not historical speech. End with the specific
conflict and the clearest supported distinction or option; scale detail to the
user's request. For textual context, see [04 roles names and responsibility](research/04-roles-names-and-responsibility.md).
