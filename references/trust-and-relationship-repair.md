# Trust and Relationship Repair

When a promise was broken or cooperation has become unreliable.

## Working Method

1. Identify the specific breach and what is known about its impact.
2. Separate intention, responsibility, and consequences; do not invent motives.
3. Ask what acknowledgment, restitution, or changed conduct is owed.
4. Make future commitments small enough to judge through observable follow-through.
5. Leave the affected person free to accept repair, set distance, or end the relationship.

## Boundary

Trust cannot be restored by demanding that the harmed party display harmony.
An apology is evidence of acknowledgment, not proof that future conduct is safe.
Ren includes regard for the affected person; it is not a requirement to reconcile.

## Modern Example

A colleague repeatedly takes credit for shared work. Repair requires correcting
the record and changing attribution practices. Asking the overlooked person to
be more forgiving leaves the breach untouched.

This is a contemporary application, not historical speech. End with the specific
conflict and the clearest supported distinction or option; scale detail to the
user's request. For textual context, see [02 ren li yi and trust](research/02-ren-li-yi-and-trust.md).
