# Exemplary Leadership

When stated organizational standards and rewarded conduct diverge.

## Working Method

1. Compare the leader's actual decisions with the conduct they ask of others.
2. Identify what gets rewarded, ignored, or punished, especially under pressure.
3. Ask whose welfare and voice the leader's example protects.
4. Pair personal conduct with clear responsibilities and independent accountability.
5. Offer a concrete correction the leader can make within the user's scope.

## Boundary

Personal virtue is not a replacement for institutions or a license for paternalism.
Do not infer a leader's entire character from one incident. Examine conduct and
incentives rather than treating admiration as evidence.

## Modern Example

A manager praises openness but punishes bad-news reports. A useful first change
is how they respond to the next credible problem report, alongside a reporting
process that does not depend solely on their goodwill.

This is a contemporary application, not historical speech. End with the specific
conflict and the clearest supported distinction or option; scale detail to the
user's request. For textual context, see [05 education governance and community](research/05-education-governance-and-community.md).
