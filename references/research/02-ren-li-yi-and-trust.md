# Ren, Li, Yi, and Trust

## Working Distinctions

These are editorial working translations, not exhaustive definitions:

| Term | Useful question | Misreading to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Ren, humane regard | How are the people affected actually treated? | Kind language automatically makes a practice humane |
| Li, ritual propriety | What shared form makes respectful interaction intelligible? | Every inherited form deserves obedience |
| Yi, appropriateness or rightness | Is this action fitting, beyond its immediate benefit? | Whatever the powerful prefer is appropriate |
| Xin, trustworthiness | Can others reasonably rely on what is promised? | Trust can be demanded without reliable conduct |

The concepts interact. A considerate intention does not excuse an unreliable
promise; a polished form does not justify a humiliating practice. Do not turn
this table into a claim that Confucius offered a modern four-part checklist.

## Available Textual Support

The captured [Doctrine of the Mean](../sources/books/doctrine-of-the-mean-wikisource.md)
connects humane relations, appropriateness, ritual distinctions, and sincerity.
Its discussion of reciprocity gives a tradition-level basis for examining whether
one demands conduct from others that one does not practice oneself.

The [Great Learning](../sources/books/great-learning-ctext.md), especially captured
sections 7 and 10–12, distinguishes responsibilities by relationship and describes
partiality as a problem for judgment. Its inherited social hierarchy remains part
of the text; a modern egalitarian application is an adaptation.

The [Analects index](../sources/books/analects-ctext.md) is a reading destination,
not passage evidence for these definitions. Verify exact Analects claims against
a passage-bearing source when they matter.

## Application and Counterexample

A team has a courteous meeting ritual but conceals errors. Li-like form alone
has not made the arrangement trustworthy. Examine whether people can report
problems, whether promises are realistic, and how mistakes are repaired.
Conversely, a blunt disagreement can preserve trust when it is accurate,
proportionate, and responsive to others' concerns.
