# Education, Governance, and Community

## What Example Can Explain

A leader or teacher conveys standards through actions as well as words. Ask
whether the conduct that receives attention and reward matches the stated ideal.
This offers a useful interpretive lens; it is not a universal empirical claim
that example is always more effective than rules or incentives.

## Supporting Material

The [Great Learning](../sources/books/great-learning-ctext.md), captured section 11,
links governing to one's own conduct and household relationships. Section 14
contrasts enabling capable people with obstructing them through envy. These are
textual resources for discussing example, moral formation, and appointments.
Their family-to-state analogy must not be silently turned into a literal model
for modern organizations.

The [SEP excerpt](../sources/articles/sep-confucius.md) describes how education,
ritual practice, and official roles shaped Confucius's reception. It also explains
why biographical details about disciples require care. The article's later
sections on virtues and the state are not included in this capture.

## Modern Institutional Test

Consider a manager who encourages error reporting but withdraws opportunities
from the first person to report an error. The gap lies in response and incentives,
not in a shortage of motivational quotations. Examine observable conduct and
possible alternative explanations before reaching a character judgment.

Personal example should work alongside fair rules, clear appeals, and correction
of mistakes. Accountability cannot depend entirely on a supposedly virtuous leader.
Likewise, education should permit questions and reasons, not just conformity to a
teacher's status. These are explicit modern design commitments of this skill.

## Where This Lens Stops

A historical ethical argument does not supply current evidence about education
outcomes, institutional performance, or policy. Those questions require relevant
contemporary information and attention to the actual context.
