# Roles, Names, and Responsibility

## The Role Problem

A role can carry different expectations for its holder, collaborators, and those
who assign rewards. Distinguish the title from actual authority, resources, and
accepted obligations. This is a modern way to examine the relation between names
and conduct; it is not evidence that ancient role ethics anticipated job design.

## Evidence and Its Limits

The [Great Learning](../sources/books/great-learning-ctext.md), captured section 7,
assigns different duties to rulers, ministers, parents, children, and social
partners. Sections 10–12 discuss partiality, example, and reciprocal standards.
The [Doctrine of the Mean](../sources/books/doctrine-of-the-mean-wikisource.md) also
examines what one asks of others in several relationships and whether one's own
conduct meets those expectations.

These texts show differentiated duties in a hierarchical setting. They do not
settle the legitimacy of a modern employment or family arrangement. The specific
Analects passage commonly associated with rectifying names is not present in the
[index capture](../sources/books/analects-ctext.md); verify its wording and context
before attributing an exact claim to it.

## Separate Three Questions

1. **What was agreed?** State the commitments instead of inferring them from a title.
2. **What makes fulfillment possible?** Check authority and resources as well as effort.
3. **Is the arrangement acceptable?** Agreement and tradition do not remove the
   need to consider fairness, consent, and the possibility of renegotiation.

## Modern Transfer

For an owner who is accountable for delivery but cannot resolve conflicting
approvals, clarify who can decide and how disagreement is handled. For family
care, distinguish a real need from an unlimited duty assigned to one person.
In either case, do not let role language conceal an unequal burden.

Long-term cooperation can be maintained through reliable obligations, but ending
or revising an arrangement can be the responsible result when reciprocity fails.
