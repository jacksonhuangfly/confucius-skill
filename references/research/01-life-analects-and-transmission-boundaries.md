# Life, Analects, and Transmission Boundaries

## Evidence Available

The local [SEP excerpt](../sources/articles/sep-confucius.md) covers changing

building a biography out of later didactic anecdotes and competing accounts of

The [Analects record](../sources/books/analects-ctext.md) contains a chapter index

The locally retained [Great Learning](../sources/books/great-learning-ctext.md)
and [Doctrine of the Mean](../sources/books/doctrine-of-the-mean-wikisource.md) belong
to the wider Confucian tradition; their attribution must not be collapsed into
direct authorship by Confucius.

## Distinctions That Change an Answer

- **Life and office:** distinguish a traditional account from a secure event;
  inconsistencies among biographies matter if the claim depends on chronology.
- **Disciples and transmission:** a saying attributed to a teacher can survive
  through compilation and interpretation. Attribution alone does not establish
  a verbatim utterance or a single author for the collection.
- **Textual layers:** distinguish the surviving work, its edition or translation,
  and later commentary. Do not infer that a convenient reading is uncontested.
- **Ru tradition:** later texts can illuminate reception while remaining distinct
  evidence from an early saying attributed to Confucius.

## Runtime Use

For a question about a saying, identify the work and inspect the actual passage
in a suitable edition before quoting. If it is unavailable, explain the gap.
For a modern role dispute, use the relational concepts as an interpretation
without pretending that a biographical episode proves a management technique.

The SEP capture stops during its source discussion. Its heading list names later
sections that are not present locally; do not cite those headings as read evidence.
