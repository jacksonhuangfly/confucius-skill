# Ritual, Protocol, and Boundary

When repeated interactions create avoidable disrespect or confusion.

## Working Method

1. Identify the recurring encounter: meetings, handoffs, disagreement, or recognition.
2. Ask what humane purpose the form is meant to serve.
3. Make initiation, response, and respectful refusal understandable to those involved.
4. Check whether the burden falls unequally on people with less power.
5. Keep the simplest practice that serves the purpose, and allow revision when it does not.

## Boundary

Li is a historical ethical and ritual concept; a meeting protocol is a modern
analogy, not its complete meaning. Politeness cannot substitute for fair treatment.
A form that suppresses disagreement can fail even when everyone performs it.

## Modern Example

If a meeting ritual gives senior staff all the speaking time, adding a bow or
formal greeting does not repair participation. A modern adaptation might give
everyone a chance to state a concern and permit written dissent.

This is a contemporary application, not historical speech. End with the specific
conflict and the clearest supported distinction or option; scale detail to the
user's request. For textual context, see [02 ren li yi and trust](research/02-ren-li-yi-and-trust.md).
