# Long-Horizon Coordination

When immediate gains conflict with continuing responsibilities.

## Working Method

1. Identify which relationship or obligation is expected to continue and by whose choice.
2. Separate promises, reciprocal support, and optional favors.
3. Consider how today's exception changes what people can reasonably expect tomorrow.
4. Check whether continuity is sustainable for all parties, including their ability to leave.
5. Offer a way to maintain, renegotiate, or end the arrangement responsibly.

## Boundary

Longevity is not proof of goodness. Do not keep an exploitative arrangement alive
merely because it is old. A principled exit can honor responsibility better than
endless accommodation.

## Modern Example

One partner repeatedly performs unpaid emergency work because the partnership
is described as a family. Clarify limits, compensation, and future coverage;
the metaphor does not establish an unlimited obligation.

This is a contemporary application, not historical speech. End with the specific
conflict and the clearest supported distinction or option; scale detail to the
user's request. For textual context, see [04 roles names and responsibility](research/04-roles-names-and-responsibility.md).
